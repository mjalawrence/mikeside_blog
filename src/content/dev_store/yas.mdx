## First Build

### August 16th

The short and sharp burst of effort at Otherside was doomed to be a deadend. I had planned to start building there on Saturday but on Thursday night I had noticed signs of the inevitable. With no intention to put money into a lost cause, I moped for the morning wondering what to do and eventually decided to scope out a pedestrianised road 1/2 a mile up river that I'd heard Pat mention in the past.

<img
  src="/images/first_build/backfill/backfill_noforms.jpeg"
  alt="backfill"
/>

I went down for a reccy, saw the existing retaining wall and the soft tarmac and the road barrier already down there asking to be backfill and decided to send it. I hauled 75 red bricks from Brickside in my car and set up the formers.

<img
  src="/images/first_build/backfill/backfill_bricks.jpeg"
  alt="bricks"
/>

There were plentiful rocks and hardcore for the small back fill. I had a wire net and some leftover heras fence for rebar.`

By 6.30pm I had the materials for concrete, water and a shovel and began mixing up on the tarmac.

<img
  src="/images/first_build/backfill/backfill_rebar.jpeg"
  alt="rebar"
/>

By 8.30pm the shaping was done. 2x ballast2:sharp1:0.5cement mixes and 1x sharp1:10mmgravel1:0.5cement mixes. The ballast mix for the body and the gravel for the coping and the transition.

The coping was shaped with a tool made from a cylindrical water bottle screwed to a bit of batten for a handle. I'd first used this improvised coping tool on the big QP at Brickside. Although it served me well, its fundamental flaw was its flimsiness and the fact I was afraid it might break mid-build.

<img
  src="/images/first_build/conned/coping_left_dark.jpeg"
  alt="coping"
/>

The concrete had gone in well and no one who passed by had been offended by me building.  
The spot was a goer.
