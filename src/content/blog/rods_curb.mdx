    <h2 class="subheader">{`
      The Lower Rods Curb Reborn
    `}</h2>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Every DIY needs a good curb. Mikeside had been blessed early on with a 2.5m curb from the NSD factory, but as the spot matured there was an obvious need for a longer one.
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    A curb that had been poured in place at Lower Rods in Leeds (RIP) was universally praised among the Sheff scene and the dimensions even shared in the main group chat in hopes that it could be recreated.
    `}</p>

    <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/spec.jpeg"/>

    <div class="carousel">
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/wholepark_before.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    At the time, after an intense 8 months of building at the spot, I was trying to force myself to not build anything but couldn’t resist casually reaching out to the original creator, Chris Parsons, just to ask for more details about the recipe he used for future reference. Not only was he forthcoming with advice but he immediately offered to come and help with the build whenever that might be.
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Somewhat tentatively, I proposed a date for a Sunday a month in the future and continued to pester him with questions about the forms, the mix and the required tools.
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    When it came to the date, we ended up delaying for a week as I’d naively assumed the forms could be quickly prepped but that was definitely not the case:
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    - Firstly I had to skate 3 3.5m lengths of thick timber by hand half a mile up the road from Wickes.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/timber.jpeg" gallery="timber" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/timber2.jpeg" gallery="timber" />
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    - Second, the 3 lengths of timber needed to be cut at a 67degree along their entire length. Me not having a circular saw, this is where Beeside’s Ashley saved the day.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/ash.jpeg" gallery="cutting" />
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/end.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/end2.jpeg" gallery="cutting" />
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/end3.jpeg" gallery="backfill" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/end4.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`Ashley and co came over from Manchester for the day bringing his circular saw and 4 batteries with which we were able to cut 1 length and half of the other 2 before the batteries gave up.
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    It was then left to me to finish the job with a hand saw (and chisel) after work during the week. This was a brutal slog that must’ve taken 2 hours, but it got done.
    `}</p>

    <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/timber_cut2.jpeg"/>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Later with Tomasz’ screw gun, the forms were strapped together and the simple task of prepping the forms was done.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/forms_assembled.jpeg" gallery="assembled" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/forms_assembled2.jpeg" gallery="assembled" />

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    **** APRIL PAINTING ****
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/april.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/april2.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}

      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/form/form_painted.jpeg" gallery="forms_painted" />

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    The next Sunday came and a heatwave was in full swing. I started the morning with a trip to wickes for 14 bags of 40n blue circle premix that Chris had requested. Premix is spenny but should guarantee the strength and uniformity desired for a slappy that is certain to take a relentless battering for the duration of its existence.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/nsd_buildday.jpeg" gallery="nsd_curbs" />
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/nsd_buildday2.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/nsdcurb.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/nsd_removed.jpeg" gallery="nsd_curbs" />
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Chris, Ben and Jake arrived after 11am and immediately started preparing the rebar and rough cutting into the tarmac ground.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/rebar.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/rebar2.jpeg" gallery="team_leeds" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/lamppost.jpeg" gallery="team_leeds" />
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/floor_cut.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    We set up the 5.2m long forms using a string line and level to keep them as straight and flat as possible. 2 bridges were screw into the top and heavy weights placed along the outside to prevent the forms bowing.
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Chris sealed the bottom edges of the forms with some hardening/expanding(?) foam and back(inner)fill was placed in the center.
    `}</p>

    <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/foaming2.jpeg" />

    <div class="carousel">
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/lamppost2.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    By 1 the concrete was being mixed with a few extra people showing up and pitching in. The moist concrete was slopped in and forced as far down into the forms as possible with a short bit of batten and a thin metal bar for a PDT.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/mixing.jpeg" gallery="mixing" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/precon/mixing2.jpeg" gallery="mixing" />
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Within an hour, 13 bags had been mixed and the forms were full; the top screeded, worked and levelled off then the outside of the forms battered a little to try to make the outer concrete settle. Then it was left to sit and go off in the heat for a while.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/concreting/conned.jpeg" gallery="conned" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/concreting/conned2.jpeg" gallery="conned" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/concreting/conned3.jpeg" gallery="conned" />
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    ****
    (nb Chris’ reassuring presence and strong leadership presence had the affect of making me complacent and forgetting that i was meant to be in charge/responsible for the outcome which led to the ridge)
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    We got on with some skating and returned occasionally to work the top with the improvised wood trowel. Once again, I dropped the ball as project leader and let some significant ridges and dips appear around the bridges where it was harder to pass the trowel underneath it and achieve uniformity (fortunately this was ironed out at a later date).
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    After the concrete was starting to harden fully, Chris got out his 67 degree trowel and shaped the edges all the way along the top of the curb on both sides, then every hour or so for the rest of the day I stroked the top with a finishing trowel.
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Before leaving, I doused the whole thing in water and then laid a sheet of thick black plastic over it, donated by Chris, and secured it with heavy weights.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/postcon/wrapped.jpeg" gallery="wrapped" />

    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    For the next 2.5 weeks it would stay like this curing, only being uncovered to be watered when possible.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/concreting/watered.jpeg" gallery="watered" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/concreting/watered2.jpeg" gallery="watered" />
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    After a patient 3 week wait, Chris returned on a Wednesday evening with his hand held grinder and assortment of brushes.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      {/* <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/postcon/popped_forms.jpeg" gallery="backfill" /> */}

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    We patched some spots with quick set concrete along the bottom edge of the curb where our poking and pushing hadn’t been thorough enough and the concrete hadn’t reached leaving unfortunate voids.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/postcon/holes.jpeg" gallery="holes" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/postcon/hole.jpeg" gallery="holes" />
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Meanwhile Chris got started with the diamond grinder disc, smoothing out the ridges and levelling the dips to achieve a more or less perfectly straight and flat top surface.
    `}</p>

    <div class="carousel">
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/postcon/smoothing.jpeg" gallery="smoothing" />
      <ImageLightBox src="/images/rod_curb/postcon/smoothing2.jpeg" gallery="smoothing" />
    </div>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    Once the curb had reached it final form, the disc was swapped for a finer grain and the buffing commenced, making the surface smooth to the point of shiny before finally a first layer of red masonry paint could go on.
    `}</p>

    <p class="journal_entry">{`
    The second coat of paint and final layers of lacquer would go on the next evening and the curb was christened.
    `}</p>
